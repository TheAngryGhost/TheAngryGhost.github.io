Overview

Province of Music is a solo project that demonstrates product thinking, systems design, and follow-through: I identified a limited audio customization workflow, built a replacement system around user-managed sample packs, documented the toolchain, and shipped it to thousands of users.

The project runs as a client-side Minecraft extension, but the core engineering work is an audio replacement layer: it takes the game's simple built-in music events and maps them to higher-quality SF2 soundfont instruments through user-managed sample packs. It also includes tooling for installing and updating packs, toggling replacement behavior, recording generated music, replaying recordings, and exporting them as MIDI files for remix or arrangement workflows.

The first major adoption bump came from a launch video I published to my YouTube channel, which helped drive the project to roughly 1.7k downloads. Since then, most growth has been organic through Modrinth search, community recommendations, forum and Reddit discussions, and third-party modpacks I did not create.

Highlights

  • Designed a custom sample-pack format for replacing simple in-game instruments with SF2 soundfont files.
  • Built a recording and MIDI export workflow for downstream editing and remixing.
  • Added install/update tooling so users can manage audio packs without manual file surgery.
  • Released, documented, and maintained the project as open-source software.
  • Kept the project current across platform updates, including newer supported 1.21.x releases.

Notable Features

Sample Pack System

A custom pack format lets users swap generated instruments with real soundfont files, showing an ability to design domain-specific data workflows instead of forcing the problem into a weaker asset pipeline.

MIDI Workflow

The engine can record generated music, replay recordings, and export them as MIDI files for remixing, arrangement, or external editing.

Usable Configuration

The configuration UI covers installing packs, selecting instruments, changing pitch, tuning volume, and saving changes, making a technically complex audio tool usable.

Adoption & Distribution

Launch Push

A self-published demo/install video gave the project its first real audience, showing that I could explain the tool clearly enough for users to try it.

Organic Discovery

Downloads continued after the launch bump through Modrinth search, community posts, and users recommending the mod when people asked how to improve the audio experience.

Third-Party Modpacks

Community-made modpacks included Province of Music without my involvement, which is useful evidence that the project solved a real enough problem for others to bundle it.

Why This Matters

  • The project moved past a personal demo into a released tool with thousands of downloads and continued use after launch.
  • Adoption depended on more than code: documentation, install flows, update commands, and configuration screens all had to work for users who never opened the source.
  • Maintaining compatibility across game updates meant revisiting install paths, settings, and audio workflows as the platform changed.
  • Community modpacks and recommendations picked it up organically, outside of the original launch video or any direct promotion.
